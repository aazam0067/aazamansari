Security incident

Investigating a compromise across WordPress files

Skills demonstrated: Security triage · Evidence collection · Escalation judgment

Escalated for specialist cleanup

Read investigation and outcome

Problem and context

Search results showed Japanese-language SEO spam associated with a customer's website, even though Google Search Console showed no security issues. The partner also reported unfamiliar plugins and a suspicious ZIP-based plugin installation. These conflicting signals required checking the website itself rather than treating the absence of a Search Console warning as reassurance.

Diagnosis and evidence

I ran a full Wordfence scan, which returned 123 findings. The findings included a configuration file flagged as malicious, a modified core index file, backdoor plugins and must-use plugins, standalone webshells, and suspicious files hidden in duplicated core folders. I reviewed the findings and documented evidence of compromise across several parts of the installation; the scan count was not a count of confirmed separate infections.

My action

Given the spread and severity of the findings, I escalated the case to the backend/security team for specialist cleanup. I advised the partner to avoid further plugin changes so the investigation evidence would be preserved.

Outcome and limits

The documented outcome was a security escalation supported by detailed findings. The supplied notes do not confirm completed cleanup or a clean post-remediation scan, so I do not present this as a fully resolved incident.

3–12 Jul 2026Complex complexity

Email authentication and delivery

Separating sender authentication from recipient-side email rejection

Skills demonstrated: Authentication troubleshooting · Escalation follow-through · Technical handoff · Customer communication

Platform fix communicated; recipient action advised; ticket closed

Read investigation and outcome

Problem and context

The partner initially reported bounced Gravity Forms notifications on 23 June, then raised an email-history visibility issue on a second website. An earlier team update described successful delivery events and a global fix for the history view. The partner briefly believed the bounces had stopped, but reported them again on 1 July. The renewed failure required separating a reporting issue from the delivery problem that was still affecting the first website.

Diagnosis and evidence

My documented involvement began on 3 July. I noted that the email was already verified in the application and checked with the team through the existing escalation. On 5 July, I followed up internally and kept the partner informed while the investigation continued. Application-level verification alone did not explain the recurring bounces.

By 9 July, the team's investigation had identified and corrected a sending-side authentication issue. In my customer update, I explained that the site's messages were now signed as the sending domain and passed DMARC. However, a second layer remained: the recipient's Mimecast security service was rejecting the messages under its impersonation/anti-spoofing policy. In this case, authentication passing did not mean the recipient's separate security policy would accept the external sending service.

My action and technical handoff

I translated the team's findings into a practical explanation of the two issues: the platform-side authentication defect had been addressed, while the remaining rejection required action from the recipient's email administrator. I explained that the website sent mail through SendGrid on the customer's behalf and that the recipient's policy was treating this as an external source using the organization's domain.

I advised the partner to have their Mimecast administrator review the identified sending source for an appropriate permitted-sender entry or impersonation-protection exception, and offered to provide the sending details needed for that review. I then moved the ticket to Waiting on Customer. This was a targeted handoff for the recipient administrator, not a claim that I had changed their security configuration.

Outcome and limits

I followed up on 12 July and marked the ticket solved. The thread documents the team-delivered authentication correction, my explanation of the remaining rejection, and the next steps provided to the partner. It does not contain confirmation that the recipient administrator applied the exception or that final delivery succeeded. Ticket closure is therefore recorded separately from verified end-to-end recovery.
